## Publication Ethics Statement

The Proceedings of the 27th IMEKO TC4 Conference 2026 with Special TC11 Technical Session are published by IMEKO – International Measurement Confederation. The publication of a peer-reviewed paper is a building block in the development of a coherent and respected network of knowledge and is evidence for the quality of the work of its authors and their institutions. It is therefore important to lay down standards of expected ethical behaviour for all parties involved in publishing: the authors, the Editor and the conference committees, the peer reviewers and the publisher.

This statement is adapted from the publishing ethics policies that Elsevier provides to journal editors through its Publishing Ethics Resource Kit ([PERK](https://www.elsevier.com/editor/perk)): the [Publishing Ethics policy](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/publishing-ethics), the [Article Correction, Retraction and Removal Policy](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/article-withdrawal) and the [Generative AI Policies for Journals](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/generative-ai-policies-for-journals), which follow the guidelines of the Committee on Publication Ethics ([COPE](https://publicationethics.org/core-practices)). The same ethical principles apply to papers from the Proceedings that are invited for extended publication in journal special issues. This statement is binding for all authors, reviewers and editors involved in the Proceedings.

### Duties of the Publisher and the Organizing Committee

**Guardianship of the scholarly record.** IMEKO, as the publisher, and the Organizing Committee support the work of the Editor and the volunteer work of the peer reviewers in maintaining the integrity of the scholarly record, and are ultimately responsible for ensuring that best practice is followed in the Proceedings.

**Systematic manipulation of the publishing process.** Systematic manipulation is not tolerated. This includes subverting or unduly influencing the submission, peer review or publication process, authorship integrity violations, citation manipulation, and fabricated, reused or implausible content. Where the burden of evidence supports suspicions that submissions or publications have been affected by such efforts, the Editor and the publisher reserve the right to reject or retract a paper or a group of papers, and may notify an author's institution, employer or funder where appropriate.

**Editorial independence.** Sponsorship, exhibition or other commercial revenue of the conference has no influence on editorial decisions.

### Duties of the Editor

**Publication decisions.** The Editor, together with the General Conference Chair and the Technical Programme Co-Chairs, is responsible for deciding which of the submitted papers are published in the Proceedings. The validation of the work in question and its importance to researchers and readers must always underwrite such decisions. The Editor is guided by the policies of the conference committees and constrained by legal requirements regarding libel, copyright infringement and plagiarism, and may confer with other committee members or reviewers in making these decisions.

**Peer review.** The Editor ensures that the peer review process is fair, unbiased and timely. Each full paper is reviewed by at least two independent reviewers with suitable expertise in the relevant field, and where necessary the Editor seeks additional opinions. The Editor reviews all disclosures of potential conflicts of interest and suggestions for self-citation made by reviewers in order to determine whether there is any potential for bias. Reviews that are hostile, unprofessional or not scientifically constructive may be edited or withheld from the authors.

**Fair play.** Manuscripts are evaluated for their intellectual content without regard to race, gender, sexual orientation, religious belief, ethnic origin, citizenship or political philosophy of the authors. Reviewers and authors are given a clear understanding of what is expected of them. Authors may appeal against editorial decisions by contacting the Editor.

**Citations.** The Editor does not require that references to particular publications be included except for genuine scholarly reasons, and authors are not required to cite the work of the Editor, the reviewers or their associates. The Editor may remove inappropriate citation suggestions from reviewer comments before sharing them with the authors.

**Confidentiality.** The Editor protects the confidentiality of all material submitted to the conference and of all communications with reviewers, and protects the identities of the reviewers. In exceptional circumstances, the Editor may share limited information with editors of other publications, institutions or organizations that investigate cases of research misconduct, where necessary to investigate suspected ethical breaches. Unpublished materials disclosed in a submitted manuscript are not used in the Editor's own research without the express written consent of the authors. Privileged information or ideas obtained through peer review are kept confidential and not used for personal advantage.

**Declaration of competing interests.** The Editor and the committee members responsible for editorial decisions are not involved in decisions about papers which they have written themselves, which have been written by family members or close colleagues, or which relate to products or services in which they have an interest. Such submissions are subject to the usual procedures, and their peer review is handled independently of the author concerned.

**Vigilance over the published record.** The Editor safeguards the integrity of the published record by reviewing and assessing reported or suspected misconduct, in conjunction with the publisher. This generally includes contacting the authors and giving due consideration to the complaint, and may include further communication with the relevant institutions and research bodies. When presented with convincing evidence of misconduct, the Editor arranges the prompt publication of a correction, retraction, expression of concern or other correction to the record.

**Use of generative AI.** The Editor does not upload a submitted manuscript or any part of it into a generative AI tool, as this may violate the authors' confidentiality and proprietary rights. AI tools cannot replace the Editor's critical thinking, independent evaluation or final decision-making. They may be used only in a supportive capacity, for example to improve the language of decision letters, under human control and oversight.

### Duties of Reviewers

**Contribution to editorial decisions.** Peer review assists the Editor in making editorial decisions and, through the editorial communication with the authors, assists the authors in improving the paper. In addition to the scientific evaluation of the manuscript, a reviewer should be alert to potential ethical issues in the paper and bring them to the attention of the Editor, including any substantial similarity or overlap between the manuscript under consideration and any other published paper of which the reviewer has personal knowledge. Any statement that an observation, derivation or argument had been previously reported should be accompanied by the relevant citation. Reviewers are asked to treat authors and their work as they would like to be treated themselves. Any reviewer who feels unqualified to review the research reported in a manuscript, or knows that its prompt review will be impossible, should notify the Editor and decline to participate in the review process.

**Standards of objectivity and competing interests.** Reviews are conducted objectively. Personal criticism of the authors is inappropriate, and reviewers should express their views clearly with supporting arguments. Reviewers should declare potential conflicts of interest to the Editor before agreeing to review a manuscript and should discuss with the Editor whether they need to recuse themselves. Examples of potential conflicts of interest include recent collaborations between the reviewer and the authors, close personal relationships, and direct competitive interests. Reviewers do not review papers of which they are authors or co-authors.

**Citations.** Reviewers should not suggest citations to their own work or to the work of their associates unless there are genuine scientific reasons. Any suggested citation must be accompanied by complete bibliographic details and a clear justification. The Editor may remove citations that are, in the Editor's view, inappropriate.

**Confidentiality.** Any manuscript received for review must be treated as a confidential document. Reviewers must not share the review or information about the paper with anyone, nor contact the authors directly without permission from the Editor. Unpublished materials disclosed in a submitted manuscript must not be used in a reviewer's own research without the express written consent of the authors. Privileged information or ideas obtained through peer review must be kept confidential and not used for personal advantage.

**Use of generative AI.** Reviewers must not upload a submitted manuscript or any part of it into a generative AI tool, as this may violate the authors' confidentiality and proprietary rights. AI tools cannot replace a reviewer's critical thinking or independent evaluation and may be used only in a supportive capacity, for example to improve the language and structure of a review report, provided that confidentiality is maintained. Reviewers remain fully responsible and accountable for the content of their review reports and should disclose any such use to the Editor.

### Duties of Authors

**Reporting standards.** Authors should present an accurate account of the work performed as well as an objective discussion of its significance. Underlying data should be represented accurately in the paper. A paper should contain sufficient detail and references to permit others to replicate the work. Fraudulent or knowingly inaccurate statements constitute unethical behaviour and are unacceptable.

**Data access and retention.** Authors may be asked to provide the research data supporting their paper for editorial review. They should be prepared to provide access to such data, if practicable, and to retain such data for a reasonable number of years after publication.

**Originality and acknowledgement of sources.** Authors should ensure that they have written entirely original works and, if they have used the work and/or words of others, that this has been appropriately cited or quoted and that permission has been obtained where necessary. Proper acknowledgement of the work of others must always be given. Information obtained privately, as in conversation, correspondence or discussion with third parties, must not be used or reported without explicit written permission from the source. Plagiarism takes many forms, from passing off another's paper as the author's own paper, to copying or paraphrasing substantial parts of another's paper without attribution, to claiming results from research conducted by others. Plagiarism in all its forms constitutes unethical behaviour and is unacceptable. Submissions may be screened with similarity-detection software.

**Multiple, redundant or concurrent publication.** Authors should not publish manuscripts describing essentially the same research in more than one primary publication. Submitting the same manuscript to more than one conference or journal concurrently constitutes unethical behaviour and is unacceptable. A paper that has been published previously should not be submitted, except in the form of an abstract, as part of a published lecture or academic thesis, or as an electronic preprint. Extended versions of Proceedings papers submitted to journal special issues must cite the Proceedings paper and must comply with the originality requirements of the journal.

**Citations.** Authors must cite relevant, timely and verified literature to support the claims made in their paper, and citations should generally be to peer-reviewed sources. Authors should ensure that statements which rely on external sources are cited accurately and should avoid excessive and inappropriate citations to their own work or to the work of other authors or institutions. If authors suspect that a reviewer has suggested citations for reasons other than genuine scientific ones, they should inform the Editor.

**Confidentiality.** Information obtained in the course of confidential services, such as refereeing manuscripts or grant applications, must not be used without the explicit written permission of the author of the work involved.

**Authorship of the paper.** Authorship should be limited to those who have made a significant contribution to the conception, design, execution or interpretation of the reported study. All those who have made substantial contributions should be listed as co-authors. Others who have participated in certain substantive aspects of the paper should be recognised in the acknowledgements. The corresponding author should ensure that all appropriate co-authors and no inappropriate co-authors are included on the paper, and that all co-authors have seen and approved the final version of the paper and have agreed to its submission for publication. Authors are expected to provide the definitive list of authors at the time of submission. Only in exceptional circumstances will the Editor consider the addition, deletion or rearrangement of authors after submission, and all authors must agree with any such change. Authors take collective responsibility for the work, and each author is accountable for ensuring that questions related to the accuracy or integrity of any part of the work are appropriately investigated and resolved. Generative AI tools cannot be listed or cited as an author.

**Institutional affiliations.** Authors should use the full, standard name of their institution and state its country, so that the affiliation can be independently verified.

**Hazards and human or animal subjects.** If the work involves chemicals, procedures or equipment that have unusual hazards inherent in their use, the authors must clearly identify these in the manuscript. If the work involves human participants or their data, or animals, the authors must state that the applicable ethical approvals and informed consent were obtained.

**Declaration of competing interests.** All authors should disclose in their manuscript any financial and personal relationships with other people or organizations that could be viewed as inappropriately influencing their work. All sources of financial support for the conduct of the research and/or preparation of the paper should be disclosed, as should the role of the sponsors, if any. Examples of potential conflicts of interest include employment, consultancies, stock ownership, honoraria, paid expert testimony, patent applications or registrations, and grants or other funding.

**Image integrity.** It is not acceptable to enhance, obscure, move, remove or introduce a specific feature within an image. Adjustments of brightness, contrast or colour balance are acceptable as long as they do not obscure or eliminate any information present in the original.

**Use of generative AI.** Authors may use generative AI and AI-assisted technologies to support tasks such as content organization and improving language and readability, but these tools must never be used as a substitute for human critical thinking, expertise and evaluation. Authors are responsible and accountable for the contents of their work, including verifying the accuracy of all AI-generated output and its sources. Such use must be disclosed in a separate declaration statement in the manuscript, giving the name of the tool, the purpose of its use and the extent of the authors' oversight. Basic checks of grammar, spelling and punctuation do not need to be declared. AI tools must not be used to fabricate results, to invent or alter underlying data, or to create or alter images that represent primary measured or experimental data. Where AI tools form part of the research method, their use must be described in a reproducible manner in the paper.

**Notification of fundamental errors.** When authors discover a significant error or inaccuracy in their own published paper, it is their obligation to promptly notify the Editor and to cooperate with the Editor to retract or correct the paper if deemed necessary. If the Editor learns from a third party that a published paper contains an error, it is the obligation of the authors to cooperate with the Editor, including providing evidence where requested.

### Corrections, Retractions and Removal

Papers that have been published in the Proceedings remain extant, exact and unaltered as far as possible. When the scientific record requires correction, this is made by a notice published on the conference website and permanently linked to the paper, providing transparency for the scientific community.

**Corrections.** A corrigendum is published where it is required to correct an error or omission, but the results and conclusions of the paper are not affected by the error. The corrigendum is drafted by the authors and is subject to the approval of the Editor, and all authors must agree to its publication. An erratum is issued by the Editor to correct an error made during the publication of the paper.

**Expressions of concern.** An expression of concern alerts readers to potential concerns that might affect the reliability of parts or all of a published paper, for example where there is inconclusive evidence of error or misconduct that has not been resolved by an investigation, or where an investigation is underway but a judgement will not be available for a considerable time. An expression of concern remains part of the permanent record. If the investigation is resolved, a notice explaining the outcome is published.

**Retractions.** A paper is retracted where the Editor and the publisher have clear evidence that the findings are unreliable, either as a result of major error or as a result of fabrication or falsification; where the paper constitutes plagiarism, including substantial overlap in the view of the Editor; where the findings have previously been published elsewhere without proper attribution, disclosure or justification; where the paper contains material or data that the authors were not authorized to publish; where copyright has been infringed or there is another serious legal issue; where the paper reports unethical research; where there is evidence of compromised peer review, fictitious authorship or citation manipulation; where the authors failed to disclose a major competing interest that would have materially affected the interpretation of the work; or where there is evidence of any other breach of this statement, including the policy on generative AI, such that the Editor has lost confidence in the results and conclusions of the paper. A retraction notice titled "Retraction: [paper title]", signed by the Editor and, if appropriate, by the authors, is published on the conference website and in a revised version of the Proceedings. The original paper is retained unchanged except for a watermark on each page indicating that it is retracted.

**Removal.** In an extremely limited number of cases, a paper may be removed from the online Proceedings: where the paper is defamatory or infringes others' legal rights and retraction is not a sufficient remedy, where it is or is expected to be the subject of a court order, or where it might pose a serious health or safety risk if acted upon. In these cases the metadata (title and authors) are retained, but the text of the paper is replaced with a notice stating that the paper has been removed for legal reasons.

### Complaints and Appeals

Complaints about the review process, allegations of misconduct and appeals against editorial decisions should be addressed to the Editor at [imeko2026@fel.cvut.cz](mailto:imeko2026@fel.cvut.cz). Allegations are handled in accordance with the COPE guidelines. The parties concerned are given the opportunity to respond, and the outcome is communicated to the complainant.
